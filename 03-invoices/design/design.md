## 1. The story we are telling

A furniture manufacturer's accounts-payable team keys in PDF invoices by hand. Not all of them — the large domestic suppliers send structured e-invoices straight into the ledger, as most Finnish B2B invoicing now works — but a residue arrives as PDF: the German fittings supplier, the Swedish fabric house, the Estonian metal shop, the small local firms, and a steady trickle of one-off suppliers. The residue is a minority of invoices and a majority of the manual effort.

The case study demonstrates the same third option as Case 2, in a second domain. An agent reads each PDF and returns a structured record; it works, and it costs money per document. An overseer watches the agent's accepted output per supplier, and once a supplier has sent enough structurally consistent invoices, writes an ordinary deterministic parser for that supplier — plain code on the PDF's text layer that runs in milliseconds with no model at run time. Repeat suppliers migrate to free parsing one by one. One-off suppliers never do, and never should: the LLM stays exactly where it earns its keep. **The AI's job is to discover the automation, not to be it — supplier by supplier**, which is word for word what the advisory page's exemplum promises. Case 2 demonstrated the mechanism on ERP report types; this case is the exemplum made literal.

Three things are new here, beyond the change of domain. The unit of learning is the supplier rather than the document type, so the automation ceiling is set by the shape of the supplier base — a long tail of one-offs that no parser will ever cover, shown honestly as a structural floor on LLM involvement rather than a defect. The input is a rendered PDF read visually by the agent, which is the form this problem actually takes in an AP inbox. And one supplier redesigns its invoice template mid-stream: its parser fails loudly, the documents fall back to the agent, and the overseer learns the new layout and issues a second parser. Case 2 demonstrated the safety net; this case also demonstrates the system maintaining itself.

**Honesty framing (decided).** The invoices are synthetic and we say so prominently. Every document is generated by seeded Python from master data we publish, against an answer key holding the true value of every field, sealed away from the parsing path. Both the LLM path and the generated parsers are marked against that key — including one planted document that both paths accept and get wrong (§4). The templates are built from documented conventions — the EU VAT directive's own content list, national tax-authority rules, and published layout standards — cited throughout `format_research.md`, sourced [S] and inferred [I] kept distinct.

**Primary reader (decided).** The buyer — CFO / COO / Head of Finance — for whom invoice handling is the most familiar automation problem there is. Technical depth present but skimmable, per the portfolio precedent.

## 2. The fictional company

**Pyökkipaja Oy** — a Lahti contract-furniture manufacturer, ~120 employees, founded 1989, making fitted furniture for offices, schools and hotels across Finland and the Nordics. The Lahti region is Finland's historic furniture cluster, which makes the setting unremarkable in the right way. (*Pyökki* = beech, *paja* = workshop; tree-name convention per the portfolio. Collision-checked 3 Jul 2026: no matching or near company found in Finnish registers; fallback candidate *Vaahterakaluste Oy* also clear; *Visakoivu Kaluste* rejected — Visu Kaluste Oy exists, laboratory furniture, Pirkanmaa.)

In-world, Pyökkipaja's AP runs on structured e-invoicing with its large domestic suppliers, as ~85% of Finnish B2B invoicing did by 2022 (`format_research.md` §3). This case study's corpus is the **PDF residue stream**: 198 invoices across calendar 2025 from the suppliers that structured e-invoicing does not reach — three foreign repeat suppliers (cross-border trade is where PDF remains the default), five smaller domestic repeat suppliers, and a tail of eighteen one-off suppliers. Against a plausible total AP volume of a few thousand invoices a year, a residue of this size is consistent with the published statistics, and it is where the manual keying actually happens.

**The shared spine — master data** (one config file):
- **Supplier master**: 8 repeat + 18 one-off suppliers, each with country, legal identifiers (Y-tunnus / HRB / org.nr / registrikood, national VAT-ID format), bank details (IBAN+BIC; Bankgiro+OCR for the Swede), payment terms, locale, currency, invoice cadence, and layout family.
- **Product catalogues**: per goods supplier, 12–25 items with units and unit-price ranges (plywood and board, fittings and slides, upholstery fabric, steel legs and frames, packaging materials, tools and consumables).
- **PO register**: purchase-order numbers for goods purchases, with dates and ordered values; service invoices carry no PO.
- **Buyer identity**: Pyökkipaja's own block (address, Y-tunnus, VAT ID FI+digits) — required on reverse-charge invoices and printed on all.

Supplier names in §3 are **provisional pending a scout collision check before Phase 2 build** (names appear on rendered documents, so they are public; Case 5 precedent — its customer-name check caught four real companies).

## 4. The designed-outcome table — the centrepiece

Every document belongs to a pre-registered class; Phase 3 evaluation is scored against this table, and the interactive page renders it with actual results overlaid.

| Class | Meaning | Count (of 198) | Designed outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R — routine** | Repeat-supplier invoices in the supplier's standard layout | 165 | LLM path for each supplier's first 6 (discovery, 48 documents); deterministic thereafter (117 documents) |
| **O — one-off** | Suppliers seen once or twice, below any learning threshold | 20 | **Stays on the LLM path, permanently.** The structural floor: automating these would cost more than it saves, and the system should know it |
| **T — template redesign** | Supplier S8's 8 post-redesign invoices (from 1 Aug) | 8 | Parser v1 **fails validation loudly** → LLM fallback (6 documents) → overseer re-learns from the new layout → parser v2 → deterministic again (2 documents). Self-maintenance, demonstrated |
| **F — fail-and-recover** | Planted breakers: a German credit note (Gutschrift, negative amounts, Oct); a Finnish credit note (hyvityslasku, Nov); a fuel-surcharge line without product code on a freight invoice (Sep); a two-page Swedish invoice with a terms attachment (Dec) | 4 | Deterministic parse fails validation → router falls back to LLM → correct result. The safety net, demonstrated per document |
| **S — silent error** | One S2 invoice (Nov) where quantity and unit price are **transposed at the source** — printed as 50 × €2,00 where the order was 2 × €50,00. The line total (€100,00), all sums and all identifiers are internally perfect | 1 | **Neither path can catch it from the document alone** — the document lies. Deterministic path accepts it; only the answer key (holding the true transaction) sees the error. The honest ceiling — and the argument for a standard **three-way match** (purchase order – goods receipt – invoice) in production, which the writeup states |

**Pre-registered headline numbers** (derived from the table; audited by hand):
- LLM path: 48 discovery + 20 one-off + 6 T-fallback + 4 F-fallback = **78 documents (39.4%)** — within the ≤80 budget set for the build.
- Deterministic path: 117 R + 2 T + 1 S = **120 documents (60.6%)**.
- Checks: 165 + 20 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 198 ✓; 78 + 120 = 198 ✓; per-supplier: S8 = 6 discovery + 6 pre-redesign deterministic + 8 T = 20 ✓; R = 178 repeat − 8 T − 4 F − 1 S = 165 ✓.
- **The long-tail exhibit**: 8 of 26 suppliers (31%) end up automated, and they carry 178 of 198 documents (89.9% of volume). The other 69% of suppliers are 10.1% of volume and stay with the LLM by design. The automation ceiling is a property of the supplier base, not of parser quality.
- Timing constraints the config must satisfy: parsers for S1–S6 live by end of April, S7 (monthly cadence — the slow learner) by end of June, S8's v2 by end of November; December runs 17 documents of which 14 deterministic (the residue: 2 one-offs + the planted Swedish F-document).
- The steady-state claim, which is the stronger one: **from each parser's birth onward, every routine invoice from that supplier costs nothing to parse**; the whole-stream share is cadence- and tail-dependent, which the case study states rather than implies. A deterministic share *lower* than Case 2's 77% is expected and is part of the story — the tail is real.

What the buyer takes away: repeat suppliers migrate to free deterministic parsing; one-offs stay with the model on purpose; a redesign heals itself in weeks without anyone writing code; and one class of error survives everything except reconciliation against what you ordered — which is why the method keeps it.

## 5. Data design

- **In-world window**: January–December 2025 (Finnish VAT rates stable at 25.5/14/10 across the window; the 14% class becomes 13.5% on 1 Jan 2026, so the window deliberately closes before that — `format_research.md` §2).
- **Files**: per invoice, a normalised PDF in `data/documents/` (named `YYYY-MM_<supplier-slug>_<nn>.pdf`), the generating HTML in `data/html/` (public, part of the rigour story), and one JSON ground-truth record in `data/answer_key/` (every schema field, plus the class label R/O/T/F/S). The answer key never enters the parsing path.
- **Volume shape**: goods invoices 3–12 lines, service invoices 1–4 lines; ~10% of documents overflow to a second page with repeated table headers (H8).
- **Generation**: pure Python, seeded (`RANDOM_SEED = 42`), all tunables in `code/config.py`; data → per-supplier HTML template → headless Chrome `--print-to-pdf` → pypdf normalisation (fixed metadata, zeroed /ID, XMP stripped). **Spike-verified byte-identical across runs** at pinned versions (Chrome 149.0.7827.201, pypdf 5.8.0 — `format_research.md` §8); the generation manifest records the Chrome version, and byte-identity claims are relative to it. The HTML and answer key are deterministic unconditionally. No LLM anywhere in data generation.
- Weasyprint (suggested in the project spec) was replaced by the Chrome route after the feasibility spike — recorded deviation, `format_research.md` §8.

## 6. Coherence rules (non-negotiable, validated by script)

1. **Arithmetic, to the cent, in the document's own currency**: quantity × unit price (less any line discount) = line total; line totals sum to the net; VAT per rate = rate × per-rate base, rounded to 2 decimals per rate; net + VAT = gross. A total that does not add destroys the demo instantly.
2. **Mandatory content**: every invoice carries every field required by Art. 226 / AVL 209 e § for its type; reverse-charge invoices carry the required mention (the words "Reverse charge", or the customary national phrasing for intra-Community supplies, per `format_research.md` §1/§4) and both parties' VAT IDs; credit notes reference their original invoice.
3. **Identifier validity**: IBANs pass MOD-97; viitenumero check digits pass 7-3-1; the Swedish OCR passes Mod-10; VAT IDs match national formats (FI = FI+Y-tunnus digits, SE = SE+orgnr+01, DE/EE lengths); Y-tunnus check digits valid [I — standard algorithm, implemented in the validator].
4. **Reference integrity**: every goods invoice cites a PO in the shared register with PO date < invoice date ≤ PO date + 45 days; service invoices carry no PO; credit-note references resolve.
5. **Supplier stability**: identity fields (name, address, VAT ID, IBAN, layout family) constant per supplier across the year — except S8's designed template redesign, which changes layout only, never identity.
6. **Dates and numbering**: invoice dates follow each supplier's cadence within 2025, business-day weighted [I]; due date = invoice date + stated terms; invoice numbers strictly increasing per supplier with plausible gaps (suppliers have other customers).
7. **Currency and locale discipline**: SEK only and always for S4; number and date formats per locale (H2/H3); one currency per document.
8. **Answer-key completeness and verbatim presence**: every schema field present in the key for every document; every string value appears verbatim in the rendered text layer (the key is the ideal reading of the document — Case 2 principle). **Documented exception: the S document**, whose key records the true transaction (2 × €50,00) rather than the printed lie — the breach is the measurement.
9. **Class-label integrity**: per-class counts in the key match §4 exactly.
10. **Grounded formatting**: every layout feature the generator emits traces to `format_research.md` §5–§6.

`code/validate_data.py` checks all ten and exits non-zero on any failure.

## 7. Pipeline design

1. **Extraction schema** (one, JSON Schema): header (parties, identifiers, dates, terms, references, bank details), line items (description, quantity, unit, unit price, VAT rate, line total), per-rate VAT summary, totals, currency. Grounded field-for-field in Art. 226 / AVL (`format_research.md` §1–§2). The same schema binds the LLM path, the generated parsers, and the answer key — one definition of "correctly parsed".
2. **LLM path**: one Sonnet subagent call per document, reading the PDF, returning schema-validated JSON under a pinned, versioned brief (Case 2's brief-drift lesson: any edit to the brief is logged). Schema failures retry once, logged and costed. Per batch: token usage and wall time recorded.
3. **Overseer**: watches accepted parses per (supplier, layout-signature) pair. After **N = 6** structurally consistent parses of a pair, it writes a deterministic parser for that supplier on the pdfplumber text layer (tokens + coordinates). **The redesign needs no special case**: S8's new layout is a new pair, and re-learning is just learning. **Activation gate**: the candidate parser must reproduce the accepted LLM output on every stored document of its pair — regression against the pipeline's own history, no answer-key access. Parsers live in `code/parsers_generated/` with provenance headers; fail-loud posture (strict layout assertions, no silent guessing).
4. **Router**: deterministic parser first where one exists for the supplier's current layout; every deterministic result is validated — schema, full arithmetic recomputation, and master-data **existence checks** (supplier known, PO number exists in the register, IBAN belongs to the supplier). Deliberately *not* checked: line-level reconciliation of quantities and prices against the PO's contents — the standard **three-way match**, which is the production recommendation the S document exists to motivate; running it here would falsify the S outcome (the flaw that forced Case 2's S-class redesign, avoided by construction this time). On any validation failure the document falls back to the LLM and the failure is logged.
5. **Evaluation**: field-level accuracy per path against the answer key; per-class scorecard against §4; cumulative cost of the hybrid stream vs the all-LLM counterfactual (both labelled estimates, §8); deterministic share per month; parser-birth timeline; and the supplier-count vs volume-share long-tail exhibit.

The overseer is structure induction over consistent examples, plus code generation, plus a regression gate — the Case 2 recipe applied one level down, to a single supplier's paper instead of a transaction type.

## 8. Cost accounting (method B — portfolio decision, restated)

The LLM path runs as Claude Code subagents inside the existing subscription; no API keys, zero marginal spend. Accuracy figures are exact (marked against the answer key). Cost figures are **estimates, labelled as such everywhere they appear**: published per-token prices applied to measured subagent token usage, presented as a band (all-input floor to all-output ceiling) with a central estimate at a stated input/output split — the Case 2 method. One honest caveat is new: reading a PDF is a vision task, so tokens per document run materially higher than Case 2's plain-text parses, for both the hybrid stream and the all-LLM counterfactual; the estimate basis states this. Latency is subagent wall-clock, labelled harness-inclusive.
